Early home and tradition

The Hanover family’s roots in Romania and Russia form part of the history of Jewish immigrant life in Baltimore. Family accounts describe an observant Jewish home in East Baltimore, with the household, its traditions and extended family forming an important part of Eli’s upbringing.

Shabbat, family gatherings and major Jewish holidays supplied continuity across generations. These recollections illuminate the atmosphere of the home without claiming a detailed record of every observance.

Baltimore’s Orthodox community

In the twentieth century, Baltimore’s Orthodox Jewish communities supported congregations, schools, kosher food businesses and networks of mutual aid. Families maintained religious practice while navigating working life and shifts in the city’s neighborhoods.

These institutions provide context for the world in which Eli and his relatives lived; individual synagogue memberships and personal relationships should not be presumed without direct documentation.

Rabbi Jacob Aaron Max

Rabbi Jacob Aaron Max (1924–2011) was a longstanding Baltimore-area religious leader associated with Liberty Jewish Center, later Moses Montefiore Anshe Emunah Hebrew Congregation. His public career is part of the wider religious history of Baltimore. The available sources do not establish a direct personal or pastoral relationship between Rabbi Max and Eli Hanover.

A complicated public legacy

Any account of Rabbi Max should also acknowledge the serious events that affected the end of his career. In April 2009 he was convicted of a fourth-degree sexual offense and second-degree assault involving a former funeral-home employee. His sentence included a suspended one-year term and probation; contemporaneous reporting records that he did not appeal. MMAE subsequently ended its formal relationship with him.

These facts are included to provide a balanced public record of the religious figure discussed here, not to connect the incident to the Hanover family.

What the family carried forward

The family’s account emphasizes the importance of religious tradition, responsibility, and the bonds between generations. In the larger story of Eli Hanover, the home and its inherited values belong alongside the maritime years and boxing halls—not as a separate spectacle, but as part of everyday life.
