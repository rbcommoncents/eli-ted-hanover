The nights that brought Baltimore to the ring.

Before the bright lights and big names, there were neighborhood gyms, fight posters, local champions, and people willing to put on a show. Eli “Ted” Hanover was part of that story—as a young boxer and, later, as a promoter.

01 / A CITY AND A SPORT

From the neighborhood ring to the promoter’s table.

Eli's relationship with boxing did not begin behind a promoter's desk. The original family website recounts his experience as a young featherweight in Baltimore and later involvement in putting on fights. The city’s boxing scene connected athletic ambition to neighborhood institutions and local entertainment.

The surviving narrative places his early boxing activity around Carlin's Park in 1939–1940. In later decades, his promotional work became associated with a different period of Baltimore boxing: the 1960s and 1970s. Those two chapters—fighter and promoter—are related, but they should not be mistaken for a single continuous career.

For Eli, the fight night was more than the main event. It involved venues, fighters, matchmaking, publicity, ticket sales, and the audience that filled the room. His life outside the ring—as a husband, father, and Baltimorean—remains just as important to this archive.
